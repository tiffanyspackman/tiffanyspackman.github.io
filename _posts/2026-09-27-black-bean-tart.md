---
date: 2026-09-27
title: Black Bean Tart
categories:
  - Mains
featured_image: /images/black-bean-tart/black-bean-tart.jpg
recipe:
  servings: 6-8 servings
  prep: 25-30 minutes
  cook: 350°F, about 40 minutes total
  ingredients_markdown: |-
    **Crust**

    * 1 ½ cups all-purpose flour
    * 1 teaspoon ground cumin
    * 1 teaspoon chili powder
    * 1 teaspoon paprika
    * ½ teaspoon salt
    * 6 tablespoons cold butter
    * 4-6 tablespoons cold water

    **Filling**

    * 2 cans (15 oz) black beans, rinsed and drained (divided)
    * 2 tablespoons sour cream
    * 1 teaspoon salt (divided)
    * 1 package (10 oz) frozen corn, thawed
    * 1 tablespoon vegetable oil
    * 1 cup chopped red sweet pepper
    * ½ cup chopped green onions
    * ¼ to ½ cup minced fresh cilantro
    * 1 to 2 jalapeno peppers, seeded and chopped
    * ¼ teaspoon pepper
    * 1 ½ cups shredded Monterey Jack cheese

  directions_markdown: |-
    **Crust**

    1. Combine the first five ingredients.
    2. Use pastry blender to mix in cold butter. Mixture should resemble coarse crumbs.
    3. Mix in just enough water until mixture forms a ball.
    4. Press dough onto bottom and sides of a greased, 9 inch, fluted tart pan.
    5. Chill for 15 minutes in the refrigerator.
    6. Line unpricked crust with double layer of foil.
    7. Bake at 350°F for 10 minutes.
    8. Remove foil; bake for 8-10 minutes longer or until golden brown.
    9. Cool on wire rack.

    **Filling**

    1. In a blender, combine 1 cup beans, sour cream, and ½ teaspoon salt. Cover and blend until smooth.
    2. Spread over crust.
    3. In a skillet, saute corn in oil until tender.
    4. Remove from the heat.
    5. Add red pepper, onions, cilantro, jalapenos, and pepper.
    6. Add cheese and remaining beans and mound over pureed beans (pan will be full).
    7. Bake at 350°F for 20-25 minutes or until cheese is melted.

    **Note:** This is a great vegetarian main dish. We don't always include the jalapenos.
---
Tracy found this one in a Taste of Home magazine back when the family had a subscription, and every issue came with beautiful food pictures. They were trying to cut back on meat around then, and this was the vegetarian recipe that looked good enough to actually make. Matt loved it so much that any time someone asked what to make for dinner he'd say bean tart — which is why some of the kids loved it and some of them hated it.
