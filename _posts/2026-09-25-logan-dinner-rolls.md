---
date: 2026-09-25
title: Buttery Dinner Rolls (Logan's Roadhouse-Style)
categories:
  - Breads
featured_image: 
recipe:
  servings: Makes 12 large rolls (doubling the recipe makes 24-30)
  prep: 2 1/2 hours, start to finish
  cook: 10-12 minutes
  ingredients_markdown: |-
    * 3 cups all-purpose flour
    * 2 1/4-ounce packages active dry yeast (4 1/2 tsp)
    * 1 tsp salt (the printed recipe says 1 tablespoon)
    * 2 tablespoons sugar
    * 1/4 cup nonfat dry milk
    * 1 1/4 cups warm water (105-115 degrees)
    * 1 large egg, slightly beaten
    * 8 tablespoons butter, melted
    * 2 tablespoons butter-flavored vegetable shortening

  directions_markdown: |-
    **Mixing**

    1. Place 2 cups flour in a large mixing bowl. Add salt, sugar, and dry milk. Set aside.
    2. In a mixing bowl combine water and egg and stir to blend.
    3. Make a well in the center of the dry ingredients. Pour water mixture into well.
    4. Mix by hand, beating 150 strokes, frequently scraping bottom and sides of bowl.
    5. Add half of the melted butter and beat to incorporate.
    6. Add remaining flour, about 1/3 cup at a time, stirring to incorporate each addition.
    7. Cover bowl with plastic wrap. Set dough in a warm, draft-free place such as oven with pan of hottest tap water on rack below.
    8. Allow batter to rise until doubled in bulk, about 1 hour.

    **Shaping**

    1. Coat cups, bottoms and sides, of a 12-cup muffin pan (or two small round pans) with butter-flavored vegetable shortening.
    2. Punch down batter (batter will be extremely soft and sticky).
    3. Drop by hand or spoonful into prepared muffin pan cups to make 12 rolls (if in pan, each roll touching the next).
    4. Brush dough with half of the remaining melted butter.
    5. Let rise, uncovered, in a warm, draft-free place until almost doubled in bulk, about 30 minutes.

    **Baking**

    1. In preheated 400-degree oven, bake rolls on middle rack 10-12 minutes (the printed recipe says 10-18).
    2. Brush tops of cooked rolls with remaining melted butter.
    3. Serve immediately. Yields 12 large rolls.
---
The pen corrections on this one are in Tracy Spackman's handwriting. Sam grew up thinking rolls baked in a muffin tin were normal — the first time I made them I kept wondering why mine looked like muffins. I'd never made a roll that wasn't round.
